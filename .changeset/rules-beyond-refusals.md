Recovery now goes past refusals. Flask endpoints record their flask-limiter rate limits, with the value read from the decorator or from the configuration constant it names (an environment default is named as such). `endpoint-inventory.md` gains a "Requests and responses" section that states, per endpoint, the inputs the handler reads, the statuses, body kinds and JSON keys it returns, and its rate limit, each cited. The product specification's §1.3 now states, per operation, what it takes, its success status and the entities it inserts, updates or deletes, and names what was not recovered; §1.5 lists the rate limits per capability. Calls through an imported submodule (`from app import alias_delete; alias_delete.delete_alias(...)`) now resolve. Writes made by assigning a column of an annotated model instance (`alias.enabled = False`) are now recovered as updates, calls on `self` reach the class's own method, and a function called in a rule's condition (`user.can_create_new_alias()`) is followed into its guarded exits, so the free-plan alias quota and the paid-plan exemption appear under the rule. On SimpleLogin: 69 endpoints with rate limits, including alias creation at 100 a day, 50 an hour and 5 a minute.
